## Why Chicago Winters Are Hard on Your Pipes

Chicago is no stranger to harsh winters. When temperatures drop below zero — which happens regularly in our city — the water inside your pipes can freeze. When water freezes, it expands, putting immense pressure on your pipes from the inside. This pressure can cause pipes to burst, leading to costly water damage.

The good news? Frozen pipes are **preventable** with the right preparation.

## 5 Tips to Prevent Frozen Pipes

### 1. Insulate Exposed Pipes

Pipes in unheated areas like basements, crawl spaces, attics, and garages are most at risk. Wrap them with foam pipe insulation (available at any hardware store) to keep them warm.

### 2. Keep a Drip Running

When temperatures drop below freezing, let a faucet **drip slowly**. Moving water is much harder to freeze than standing water. Even a trickle can prevent a burst pipe. Leave the cabinet doors under sinks open to allow warm air to circulate.

### 3. Seal Drafts

Check for drafts near pipes — especially around windows, doors, and foundation cracks. Caulk or spray foam any openings that let cold air in. Pay special attention to areas where pipes enter your home.

### 4. Disconnect Garden Hoses

Before the first freeze, disconnect and drain all garden hoses. Close the indoor shut-off valve to outdoor spigots and leave the outdoor spigot open to allow any remaining water to drain.

### 5. Keep Your Home Heated

Never set your thermostat below 55°F when you're away from home. If you're traveling during winter, have someone check on your home daily to ensure the heat is working.

## What to Do If a Pipe Freezes

If you turn on a faucet and only a trickle comes out, you may have a frozen pipe. Here's what to do:

1. **Keep the faucet open** — As the pipe thaws, water needs to escape.
2. **Apply gentle heat** — Use a hair dryer, heating pad, or warm towels. **Never use an open flame** (blowtorch, propane heater).
3. **Check for cracks** — Once the pipe is thawed, check for any cracks or leaks.
