## How Long Do Water Heaters Last?

The average water heater lasts **8-12 years**. If yours is approaching or past this age, it's time to start thinking about replacement. In Chicago, our hard water can shorten this lifespan significantly.

## 7 Warning Signs It's Time for a Replacement

### 1. Age: Over 10 Years Old

If your water heater is over a decade old, it's living on borrowed time. Even if it's still working, efficiency has decreased significantly. Newer models can save you 20-30% on your energy bills.

### 2. Rusty or Discolored Water

If you turn on the hot water and see rusty or brown water, your tank is corroding from the inside. This means the tank lining has failed, and a leak is imminent.

### 3. Strange Noises

Popping, rumbling, or banging sounds coming from your water heater are signs of sediment buildup. As sediment hardens, it traps water underneath, causing it to boil and make noise. This reduces efficiency and eventually damages the tank.

### 4. Leaks Around the Unit

Any water pooling around your water heater is a red flag. A leaking tank usually can't be repaired — it needs to be replaced. Don't wait for a catastrophic failure.

### 5. Inconsistent Water Temperature

If your shower goes from hot to cold unpredictably, or you run out of hot water faster than you used to, your water heater is failing. This is often caused by sediment buildup or a failing heating element.

### 6. Higher Energy Bills

An aging water heater works harder to heat the same amount of water. If your energy bills are creeping up without explanation, your water heater may be the culprit.

### 7. Visible Rust or Corrosion

Check the exterior of your water heater for rust, corrosion, or moisture around the fittings and connections. These are signs that the unit is deteriorating.

## Tank vs. Tankless: Which Is Right for You?

**Traditional Tank Water Heaters:** Lower upfront cost, simpler installation, reliable technology. Good for families who use hot water at predictable times.

**Tankless Water Heaters:** Higher upfront cost but 24-34% more energy efficient. Unlimited hot water on demand. Takes up less space. Better for energy-conscious homeowners.

**Hybrid Heat Pump:** Most efficient option (up to 60% savings). Higher upfront cost but qualifies for rebates. Best for homes in moderate climates like Chicago's.
